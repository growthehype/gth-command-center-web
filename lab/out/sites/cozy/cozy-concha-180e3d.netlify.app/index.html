Tax Planning

Corporate Tax Planning

We structure how companies are owned, financed and reorganized, so that profits are taxed once, at the right rate and at the right time.

Clients often come to us when

  • You are adding a holding company and want to move surplus cash out of the operating company without triggering tax.
  • Your company is growing and you want family members or key employees to share in that growth without running into TOSI.
  • You are reorganizing a group of companies before a sale, a financing or a change in ownership.

Personal Tax Planning

We help business owners, professionals and families hold investments, share income and pass on wealth at the lowest tax cost the law allows.

Estate Planning

An estate plan decides how much tax is paid on death, and by whom. We build plans that let the business, the farm or the portfolio pass to the next generation intact.

Clients often come to us when

  • You own a business and want future growth to belong to your children while you keep control.
  • You want to provide for a child or relative with a disability without affecting their benefits.
  • You are 65 or older and considering an alter ego or joint partner trust.

Tax Litigation

05 Privilege

Privilege starts with the first call.

What you tell an advisor about a tax problem can be demanded by the CRA. What you tell your lawyer to get legal advice is protected by solicitor-client privilege, from the first conversation.

Call us in confidence
You
AccountantTax lawyerPrivileged
CRA3 documents
Requirement s. 231.2

Accountants are essential to your file. Their conversations with you are not privileged.

  1. 01No accountant-client privilege. Canadian law does not protect communications between a taxpayer and an accountant from disclosure. Tower v. MNR, 2003 FCA 307
  2. 02The CRA can require the file. A requirement can compel an accountant to produce working papers, emails and notes about your affairs. ITA s. 231.2
  3. 03Planning can become evidence. What was said while a transaction was being planned can be read by the auditor reviewing it.

Advice from your lawyer is privileged. The privilege belongs to you.

  1. 01Protected from the start. Communications with your lawyer made to seek or give legal advice are protected by solicitor-client privilege, from the first conversation. ITA s. 232
  2. 02Beyond the reach of a requirement. The CRA’s requirement powers do not reach privileged communications. Only you can waive the privilege. Canada (AG) v. Chambre des notaires du Québec, 2016 SCC 20
  3. 03Accountants can work inside it. When we retain an accountant to help us advise you, their work for us can, in the right circumstances, fall within the privilege.

Privilege protects the advice, not the facts. Records you already had, and the transactions themselves, can still be required.

7 of our lawyers are also Chartered Professional Accountants. When they advise you as your lawyers, that advice is privileged.
